# Code of conduct

This is a small technical project with one maintainer. The expectations are
short, and they are the ones that actually matter for work like this.

## Expected

**Argue about the numbers, not the person.** This project exists to match a
reference implementation. Disagreements are settled by MATLAB line numbers,
test cases and measurements — not by seniority, tone or persistence.

**Say what you actually measured.** "I ran X and got Y" is worth more than "this
looks wrong". If you did not check, say you did not check.

**Accept being wrong quickly and without ceremony.** Everyone working on this
has been wrong about something in it, including the maintainer, repeatedly and
in writing. Correcting yourself costs nothing here.

**Assume the other person read the docs and still has a point.** If a question
seems obvious, the documentation may be at fault.

## Not acceptable

- Personal attacks, demeaning comments, or dismissing someone rather than their
  argument.
- Harassment of any kind, including unwanted contact after being asked to stop.
- Comments about someone's identity, background, employer or perceived skill
  level in place of engaging with what they said.
- Deliberately wasting maintainer time: reports known to be false, or pressure
  to change the port ahead of the Reference Code.

## Scope

This applies in issues, commit messages and any other discussion on this
repository. It does not extend to IEEE 802.3 meetings, the reflector, or any
other venue — those have their own rules, and this document does not attempt to
speak for them.

## Enforcement

Report a problem by opening an issue, or by contacting the maintainer directly
if the issue tracker is not appropriate for it.

The maintainer decides, and the range of responses is: a request to change
behaviour, editing or removing a comment, closing a thread, or blocking an
account from the repository. Reports will be handled discreetly. There is no
committee and no appeals process — this is a one-person project, and pretending
otherwise would be dishonest.
